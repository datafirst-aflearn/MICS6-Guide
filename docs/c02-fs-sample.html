2.2 Who is in the fs file?

The fs file is sometimes loosely described as the “children aged 5–17 file”. That shorthand obscures two facts:

  1. only one eligible child aged 5–17 is randomly selected per sampled household for the questionnaire; and
  2. the Foundational Learning Skills assessment itself targets children aged 7–14, not everyone in the fs file.

UNICEF documents the 5–17 questionnaire as being administered for one randomly selected child aged 5–17 in each household. The standard questionnaire then routes children aged 5–6 and 15–17 around the FLS module, leaving children aged 7–14 eligible for the learning assessment. See the MICS6 Manual for Statistical Data Analysis and standard MICS6 Questionnaire for Children Age 5–17.

2.2.1 From household members to the FLS sample

Sampled household
      │
      ▼
List all household members
      │
      ▼
Identify children aged 5–17
      │
      ▼
Randomly select one eligible child
      │
      ▼
Create an fs questionnaire/record
      │
      ├──────── age 5–6 ────────► no FLS assessment
      │
      ├──────── age 7–14 ───────► eligible for FLS assessment
      │
      └──────── age 15–17 ──────► no FLS assessment

2.2.2 The fs file is not a census of all children aged 5–17 in sampled households

A household can contain more than one child aged 5–17, but only one is selected for the questionnaire.

Household A
   child age 8    ┐
   child age 11   ├── one randomly selected for fs
   child age 16   ┘

You therefore cannot simply merge the fs file onto every child aged 5–17 in hl and treat the result as if all children had been assessed.

The selection process is reflected in the survey weight for the 5–17 questionnaire.

2.2.3 Use the fs weight for analyses whose observations are selected children

MICS provides questionnaire-specific survey weights. For the selected child file, the standard weight is fsweight.

If the outcome is foundational reading or numeracy and the analysis dataset consists of fs records, the relevant weight is the fs weight, even if you merge household or household-member characteristics onto those records.

Outcome: foundational numeracy       from fs
Age: child age                       from fs/hl
Wealth quintile:                     from hh

Unit of analysis: selected child
Weight: fsweight

The source of a covariate does not determine the survey weight. The analysis population does. See Which MICS weight should I use?.

2.2.4 The FLS target population is children aged 7–14

The MICS6 FLS module directly assesses reading and numeracy among children aged 7–14. UNICEF describes the module as measuring skills expected at approximately Grade 2/3 level in a household-survey setting.

This age range is one of the major analytical strengths of MICS. Researchers can examine foundational skills among children currently attending school, among children who have never attended, among children who attended previously but are no longer in school, by age rather than only grade, across grades, and in relation to household and family characteristics.

Because MICS is household based, the assessment is not restricted to children who are present in school on an assessment day.

2.2.5 Being age 7–14 does not guarantee a completed assessment

Age determines eligibility for the FLS module, but not every eligible child necessarily completes every assessment task.

The reading module contains several routing decisions. A child may, for example:

  • not receive permission to participate;
  • not assent to participate;
  • be routed away from the reading passage because an appropriate assessment language is unavailable;
  • decline to read;
  • not progress through the practice items where these apply; or
  • begin the assessment but not complete it.

These cases should not automatically be treated as equivalent missing values.

This is one reason AFLEARN constructs a reading_status variable that makes the reading pathway explicit. The assessment sequence is in Understanding the MICS6 reading assessment; the research-ready construction is in Constructing research-ready reading variables.

2.2.6 Age, grade and the official indicators are different concepts

MICS allows researchers to describe foundational learning for several populations.

UNICEF tabulations distinguish between children aged 7–14, children of the expected age for Grade 2/3, and children actually attending Grade 2/3. These denominators answer different questions.

A child aged 10 in Grade 2 can contribute to a grade-based Grade 2/3 estimate even though they are older than the expected age for that grade. Conversely, a child of the expected Grade 2/3 age may be in a different grade or out of school.

Always state your denominator

“Percentage with foundational reading skills” is incomplete unless the reader knows among whom: all children aged 7–14, children of Grade 2/3 age, children attending Grade 2/3, or another explicitly defined population.

The worked examples show how to construct and report these populations transparently.
